# **1\. The Problem**

**What is the customer pain or opportunity?**

Saroj runs a Find/Replace to fix a recurring typo. Because his search matches text that sits inside a marker, the replace silently strips the surrounding USFM markers (\\p, \\v, \\x, \\xo) from the passage — and there is no Undo for Replace All, so the loss is permanent. For a low-tech-preference translator, this is the worst possible outcome: the tool quietly destroyed his work.

The same day, a toolbar button he used yesterday is simply gone because his window is narrow; a menu renders half-transparent and unreadable; and a Send/Receive shows two messages at once that seem to contradict each other. None of these is catastrophic alone, but together they tell Saroj the software cannot be trusted — the exact opposite of what Paratext 10 Simple is for.

**Who has this problem?**

Primarily Saroj (low-tech-preference Simple translators), for whom unpredictability is disproportionately costly. Donna (Power) hits the same Find/Replace, Send/Receive and BCV issues, and the destructive replace endangers any user’s project regardless of mode. All items here are both-modes.

**How do we know?**

The consolidated UX-findings list and open Jira: the destructive replace and missing Replace-All undo (Find & Replace area; Replace UI is PT-3883), contradictory/failed Send/Receive status (PT-3978, PT-3975), BCV creating a stray book at 0:0 and stale availability messages (PT-3565, PT-4111, PT-4113), and the transparent menu (PT-4101). The destructive replace is a reported, reproducible data-loss scenario, not a hypothetical.

**What happens if we do nothing?**

Sooner or later a translator loses real translation work to an un-undoable Replace All — a data-loss incident with no recovery possibility (for Saroj). Meanwhile Saroj’s trust keeps eroding through the smaller papercuts, and because none of these is owned by an epic before S91, nothing on the roadmap is expected to catch them.

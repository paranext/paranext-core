# Keep subtexts short — or leave them out

A **subtext** is the secondary line of text under a heading, label, option, menu item, or setting
that explains it. Long subtexts are not welcome. The best subtext is usually **no subtext**.

## The principle: show less at a time

Show the user only the information relevant to the decision in front of them, and nothing more.
Every extra line is something to read, skip, or wade through before getting to the control.

## Why

- **Many users never read it.** Some users always skip secondary text. For them a subtext is noise
  that pushes the controls apart and adds nothing.
- **Others get stuck in it.** Users who do read can have their focus caught by a long sentence, or
  by a line that wraps and breaks their eye flow down the list. They spend effort reading the
  explanation instead of making the choice.
- **It costs space.** Every subtext line takes vertical room, so fewer options fit on screen and
  more scrolling is needed — which matters most in the narrow panels Paratext is used in (see
  <LinkTo title="Guidelines/Responsiveness">Responsiveness</LinkTo>).
- **It hides the important ones.** When every option carries a subtext, the one that really needs
  it stops standing out.

## The rule

1. **Default to no subtext.** Add one only when a user would make a wrong choice without it *and*
   none of the alternatives below can carry the message.
2. **If you must, keep it to one short line.** A phrase, not a paragraph — aim for a few words, and
   never more than one line at the component's normal width. No second sentence.
3. **Never use a subtext to restate the label** in more words.

## Alternatives, in order

Reach for these first. They follow the ladder in
<LinkTo title="Guidelines/Providing Help">Providing Help</LinkTo>.

1. **A better label.** Most subtexts exist because the label is vague. Rename the control so it says
   what it does, using the words the user already knows (see
   <LinkTo title="Guidelines/Vocabulary">Vocabulary</LinkTo>).
2. **A default that needs no explanation.** If the safe, common choice is preselected, most users
   never need to understand the alternative.
3. **Progressive disclosure.** Put the explanation one step away, where only the user who wants it
   pays the reading cost:
   - an **info icon button** with a tooltip for one or two sentences,
   - a **dialog** or **application help** for anything longer.
4. **Remove the option.** If an option needs a paragraph before anyone can choose it, question
   whether it should be offered here at all.

## When a subtext is the right call

A short subtext is still the right tool when the clarification must be visible at a glance — for
example, naming a non-obvious consequence ("Cannot be undone") or telling apart two settings whose
labels cannot be made distinct. Keep it to one muted line and wire it to the control with
`aria-describedby`, as described in
<LinkTo title="Guidelines/Providing Help">Providing Help</LinkTo>.
